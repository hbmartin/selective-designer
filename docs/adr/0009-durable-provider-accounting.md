# Capture provider accounting independently of output publication

Durably capture each performed provider request before publishing its usable output, then deliver the accounting to the cost log independently. Render and cutout checkpoints share a transaction with their receipts; automatic and interactive segmentation capture receipts before mask processing. A receipt still commits when its output loses the publication fence. Cost-log failure must not discard paid output or require another model call.

Receipts use provider, endpoint and native request identity when available, otherwise an invocation identity allocated before the provider call. Logical attempts and concepts are attribution, not deduplication identities. Success is immutable; accounting replay cannot replace a winning request's amount or regress its terminal status. Native request identity remains separate from provider polling URLs. Receipts retain original timestamps, unknown costs and attribution, have no cascading parent lifetime, and contain neither output URLs nor bytes. They do not protect storage objects from cleanup.

Capture only persists receipts. Output publication and interactive responses never await accounting queue dispatch. Render workflows dispatch on exit after publication or an unresolved/failed outcome; submitted receipt capture never waits for dispatch before polling. Other workflow steps own post-operation dispatch; HTTP facades scope every exposed operation and retain dispatch through public Vercel waitUntil in deployed preview/production requests, independent of deadline metadata. Other hosts skip prompt dispatch safely and require operator or externally scheduled reconciliation. Queue dispatch has a five-second observation deadline; late acceptance is harmless, and a failed or interrupted dispatch leaves the daily backstop responsible. Delivery uses short transactions with a one-second lock wait and five-second statement/idle limits: lock a pending receipt, write the idempotent cost record, and acknowledge delivery together. Failure leaves it pending. Prompt bounded workflow retries are backed by a daily 07:00 UTC reconciliation workflow and an operator command. Reconciliation pages contain 100 receipts with five continuously replenished worker slots per workflow: a slow delivery occupies one slot without holding back the others. Snapshot pagination and isolated failures are preserved. The cron endpoint requires its own secret; ordinary sessions do not authorize it. Production runtimes require a private secret; previews may omit it to disable the endpoint, and deployments reject the public local example.

A render journals provider acceptance in a durable workflow step before a separate persistence step captures its submitted receipt and saves the fenced submission. Persistence retries reuse the accepted response and cannot buy another request. Transient persistence errors use durable backoff until the original 30-minute owner Retry deadline, without another provider submission. Exhaustion leaves acceptance pending or submitted and owner-retryable; deterministic identity and validation errors fail the owned attempt immediately with safe diagnostics. Neither application outcome establishes provider failure. The response-before-durable-workflow-record window remains: a crashed provider step may execute again. No pre-submission reservation is used.

Every bounded recovery write for a deterministic application failure retains the original reason and submission-failure fence; a secondary database error cannot turn it into an ordinary request-matched failure or erase confirmed capture. Computed deadline validation returns a permanent application error before provider access when the deadline cannot be represented. Normal polling relies on the pinned SDK’s public sleep contract; defensive wait reconciliation remains limited to uncertain submission persistence. Native-driver timestamp regressions remain an explicit local verification step rather than CI coverage.

Accepted requests that lose the attempt fence still receive independent receipts. Submitted receipts advance on provider-confirmed failure or success; application failures do not establish provider failure. Success is canonical and immutable; repeated provider failure retains its first completion time, and a later confirmed success may advance it. Capture reconciliation verifies attribution, original start time and the required accounting state, not identity alone. Submitted work that is replaced may retain unavailable final cost; no independent polling system is introduced for abandoned requests.

The receipt is durable evidence, not exactly-once provider execution. For render submissions, loss before the durable workflow handoff can lead to another charge; for cutout and SAM calls, the response-before-receipt window remains. A database outage can prevent capture and publication. Manual Retry intentionally starts a fresh potentially paid attempt. No historical accounting, output registration or terminal URL backfill is performed.

The render recovery cutoff remains 30 minutes, with one initial persistence attempt even for late acceptance. Receipt capture is confirmed independently of the fenced submission write and retained as serializable progress. Only confirmed receipts are dispatched for delivery. An uncertain capture at the cutoff is logged explicitly and may remain solely in the workflow journal: delivery and daily reconciliation cannot create an absent receipt. No separate capture recovery runs after the deadline. Invalid accounting identity or timestamp remains terminal for automatic recovery. Malformed recovery-deadline metadata does not prevent the initial fenced save of a valid accepted request. After failure, only the matching owned attempt’s persisted start time may restore the original cutoff; successful submission reconciliation continues the same request. Unknown submission writes remain distinct from confirmed rejection and receipt capture, and their uncertainty survives later errors. A failed reconciliation or expired cutoff cannot justify failing a possibly committed request. Neither outcome establishes provider failure. Diagnostic wrappers preserve internal context and the last useful typed failure code, while serialized workflow outcomes retain only safe codes, retryability and capture confirmation.

Factories bind operation definitions once and apply isolated accounting dependencies per invocation. HTTP and workflow dispatch ownership remains unchanged; concurrent operations cannot share mutable receipt-tracking state. Accounting and cleanup reconciliation share the same deterministic replenished worker policy.

Diagnostic steps journal only execution identity, the last known typed code and current safe cause diagnostics. Internal wrapper metadata and exposure policy remain intact; transport outcomes still expose only safe fields. Cleanup intervention reporting distinguishes retained historical evidence from outstanding operator work, and audited acknowledgment never changes accounting or storage references.

The repository reports a single-snapshot matching submission; the application derives receipt-capture confirmation through the production capture-before-save invariant; supersession alone does not. Confirmation is monotonic across lost replies and defensive handling of interrupted durable waits. Before ending that defensive path, reconcile an uncertain write using the same bounded read-only mechanism; a matching save confirms capture and continues polling, including after the original cutoff. Unavailable reads preserve existing confirmation without creating another persistence window. Reconciliation uses the SDK's bounded three attempts and five-second retry delays, including read-only checks after the original cutoff, without extending submission persistence or purchasing another request. Only confirmed receipts dispatch. Diagnostic steps receive identifiers and safe fields only, preserving the previous useful typed failure code independently of the latest cause; they do not journal the full acceptance again.

Submission persistence and reconciliation share application-owned capture interpretation; factories only bind those operations and transport serializes their evidence. Successful capture remains confirmed even if the subsequent fenced submission is superseded or fails. Reconciliation infers capture only from a completely matching saved submission. Polling relies directly on the pinned SDK’s normal durable suspension/resume contract, without a separate interruption outcome. Submission-persistence wait recovery remains defensive around uncertain writes. Confirmed accounting dispatch on workflow exit and the original 30-minute owner Retry policy remain unchanged; manual Retry starts a fresh potentially paid attempt.

Failure-write recovery keeps the original reason and fences in one immutable context, with workflow-owned backoff for at most two minutes and no SDK retries inside the individual write step. Confirmed failure replay is success; unconfirmed persistence is an internal unresolved outcome and leaves the saved attempt unchanged. Confirmed receipts still dispatch on exit, including after permanent write errors, diagnostic failures or recovery exhaustion. Failure diagnostics initialize independently of rendering/database composition, retain safe cause codes and capture confirmation, and never include provider locators or raw exception messages.

Five minutes of future-clock skew is allowed for active attempt starts. Invalid or implausibly future journal deadlines cannot extend persistence recovery: valid acceptance still receives its initial capture/save, then only the matching persisted start may restore the original cutoff. A completely matching saved submission continues recovery before this time validation. Structurally valid historical terminal records remain readable, and corrupt records require operator repair.
